\section{Model}
This section outlines a simple framework that rationalizes price differences across
buyer groups and clarifies how differences in costs and demand elasticities generate
those differentials.

\paragraph{Environment.}
A seller sets a price $p$ for a good that can be sold to either of two buyer groups
($g \in \{A, B\}$). Let the seller's marginal cost of supplying the good be $c$. Serving
group $B$ may entail an additional per-unit cost $k \ge 0$ (e.g., customization,
compliance, delivery), so effective marginal costs are
\[
c_A = c, \qquad c_B = c + k.
\]
Let $D_g(p; s_g)$ denote the seller's residual demand from buyer group $g$, where $s_g$
summarizes market conditions and outside options faced by that group.

The seller chooses price to maximize expected profits:
\[
\pi_g(p) = (p - c_g)\, D_g(p; s_g).
\]
The optimal price satisfies the standard markup condition:
\[
p_g^*(s_g) = c_g + \mu_g(s_g),
\]
where $\mu_g(s_g)$ is the markup determined by the elasticity of residual demand for
group $g$. Differences in equilibrium prices across buyer groups can therefore arise from
(i) differences in marginal costs $k$, and/or (ii) differences in demand elasticities and
resulting markups.
